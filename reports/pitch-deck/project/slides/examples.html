SAME MOVE, ANY SUBJECT

Pip believes something wrong. The kid fixes it with a why.

MissionPip believesThe kid teaches
Triangle area · gr 6A = b × h, so a 6 by 4 triangle is 24“2 triangles make a rectangle, so it’s half”
Trig ratios · gr 9a bigger triangle has a bigger sine“same angles, same shape zoomed in, so the ratio stays”
sin² + cos² · gr 10sin x + cos x = 1“the sides squared add up to the long side squared, so it’s 1”
SAT writing“Running to the bus, my backpack fell open.”“the phrase describes whatever comes right after the comma”

Never the obvious, never the unprovable: definitions and laws like the Pythagorean theorem or F = ma get applied, not explained. “That’s the theorem” counts.

The same move works across subjects. In sixth-grade geometry Pip thinks a triangle’s area is base times height. In ninth-grade trig it thinks a bigger triangle has a bigger sine, and the kid explains it’s the same shape zoomed in. For the identity, Pip forgets the squares. In SAT writing Pip writes a dangling modifier and genuinely believes the backpack was running. And Pip knows the difference between a why and a rule: it never asks a kid to prove the Pythagorean theorem or Newton’s laws, it asks them to use them.
