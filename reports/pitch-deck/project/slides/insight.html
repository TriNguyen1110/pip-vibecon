These studies support the mechanism, not Pip itself, and we say so on the slide. A meta-analysis of 28 studies found preparing to teach and then teaching beat plain studying, Hedges’ g of 0.56; the abstract doesn’t specify ages. In a lab study with 180 college students, recall practice left 61 percent remembered after a week versus 40 percent for rereading. And in a high-school credit-recovery field experiment, weekly one-sentence teacher messages to parents cut failure to earn credit from 15.8 to 9.3 percent, mostly by preventing dropouts. Pip combines teaching, recall and messages home; whether it works for tutoring-center students is what a pilot measures.
