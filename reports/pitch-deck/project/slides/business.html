The business case is simple. Pip costs four dollars per student a month, so a 120-student center pays 480 dollars. In the demo center one family’s 12-hour package is 660 dollars, so keeping a single family that would have left more than pays for the month. And the owner sees it live: after Maya’s K.O., revenue at risk drops from 7,040 to 6,380 dollars. We’re honest that the price is a hypothesis and the center data is synthetic; there’s no public churn benchmark for tutoring centers, which is exactly what free pilots will measure.
