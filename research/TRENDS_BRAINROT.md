# Brainrot, TikTok and Gen Alpha (ages ~10–15): what's trending and why (as of 24 Oct 2026)

Researcher report, saved by the coordinator. Sources are numbered at the end; inferences are marked.

## Where things stand
- **Skibidi Toilet** (Feb 2023; ~23M subscribers / 9B views within six months [10]); "skibidi" now mostly a classroom word for "strange" [4].
- **Italian brainrot** (Tralalero Tralala Jan 2025, Tung Tung Tung Sahur Feb 2025, …) spread to coins, stickers, trading cards [1].
- **Roblox "Steal a Brainrot"** peaked ~25.4M concurrent (Oct 2025), ~130K concurrent by mid-Oct 2026 [2][3].
- **Decline:** brainrot Fortnite skins (Apr 2026) became the lowest-rated in the game's history, largely over AI origins [1][14]. Peak put at late 2025 – early 2026 [5].
- **"6-7"** outlasted the creatures (Dictionary.com 2025 Word of the Year), but kids still saying it get mocked by 2026 [4][6].
- **Backlash:** teen-led "Great Meme Reset of 2026" against AI/brainrot memes [7].
- *Inference:* original brainrot characters read as "little-kid / 2025" to most 13–15-year-olds; no single new character franchise is clearly trending in Sep–Oct 2026.

## Why it spreads
Absurdity is the joke ("the joke is that there is no joke"; serious delivery of nonsense) [5][11] · chantable,
rhythmic names + one recognizable sound [11][12] · lore and power-rankings built by the group, "participatory
digital folklore" [12][15] · decompression from pressure to be productive [16] · language that shuts adults out [4] ·
easy to join and remix [6][11] · novelty and fast reward [11].

## What makes a character meme-able (inference from [5][12])
Silhouette readable as a 32px sticker · one absurd mashup (animal + object) · a chantable two-part name · one
signature sound · one prop or verb · expressive face on a plain body · low-fidelity looks native; polish reads as an ad.

## What adults and brands get wrong
Arriving after the peak [17] · "how do you do, fellow kids" slang [6][17] · cute-plush corporate mascots (Meta's
"Jolly", called a "Clippy moment") [18] · AI-slop look [7][14]. Duolingo's owl is the widely cited good example [17].

## Lessons for Pip
1. Build for absurdity, not trends: no 6-7 / skibidi / aura in copy; make the character the in-joke.
2. Chantable two-part names + one sound sting for bosses.
3. Silhouette first; one mashup + one prop; 5+ screenshot-able reaction faces.
4. Deadpan, overly serious boss: grand formal villain lines about trivial nonsense; loses with dignity.
5. Leave lore gaps; a power-ranking ladder of minor villains kids can rank and name (moderated).
6. Hand-made, slightly scrappy look; avoid the generic AI look and corporate cute-plush.
7. Optional collect / defend / steal-back meta-loops around lessons.
8. Self-deprecating mascot; boss may mock the app; no meanness or edgy content.
9. Let kids lead; never explain the joke.
