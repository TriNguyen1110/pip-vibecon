# What 10–15-year-olds love in characters (2025–26), and why

Researcher report, saved by the coordinator. **[I]** = researcher's inference. Several stats are vendor-commissioned
(Crunchyroll, Roblox, UserTesting); the Halogen poll is Singapore-only.

## Evidence highlights
- **Anime is mainstream for teens:** ~60% of 13–17-year-olds call themselves fans; ~40% of teen fans say it's part of
  who they are [1]. Demon Slayer leads with kids (Infinity Castle: biggest US anime opening, $600M+ worldwide) [2][3].
  Chiikawa won the 2025 Japan Character Awards Grand Prix: anxious characters who fail at small things and stay kind [4].
- **Games/avatars are identity:** Gen Alpha's top topics are Roblox, Minecraft, Fortnite, GTA, Pokémon [5]. ~274M
  Roblox avatar edits a day; 73% want avatars that express a full range of emotions [7]. Default skins ("bacon hair")
  get mocked; rare/detailed items earn status; kids match outfits with friends [8]; some are picked on over skins [9].
- **Collectibles:** Pop Mart 2025 revenue ~$5.4B, Labubu ~$1.95B [11]. 58% of 13–16-year-olds own a blind box;
  "cool/cute design" 65%, excitement of opening 48% [12]. Ugly-cute = soft body + sharp teeth; Labubu is a "blank IP"
  fans project onto [14]. Kuromi's "dark kawaii" draws kids who feel at odds with the mainstream [16]. Kawaii isn't
  limited to baby-like features [17].
- **Nostalgia:** kidults ~31% of UK toy spending [18]; Gen Z plush tied to loneliness and community [19]; "2026 is the
  new 2016" [22]. [I] For 10–15-year-olds, childhood ≈ 2014–2020: early Minecraft, FNAF, Among Us, Pokémon GO.
- **Action/superheroes are slipping:** only 4 of Gen Alpha's top 20 franchises [5]; 59.7% of 10–24-year-olds want
  friendship-centered stories [23]. Participatory spectacle wins (KPop Demon Hunters, "chicken jockey") [24][25].
  Games motivate most when you feel like your ideal self [26].
- **Gender:** creatures over humans, round bodies, muted or black + bright palettes, mischief over sweetness or
  toughness read neutral [28]; labeling colors by gender widens gaps [28].

## Design principles for a mascot 10–15-year-olds would actually like
1. Recognizable silhouette: one round chunky shape + one odd feature; reads as a tiny emoji or bag charm.
2. Ugly-cute contrast: soft body + one slightly off detail (fangs, smirk, tired eyes). Pure sweet reads "little kid".
3. Many expressions (≥ 8: smug, panic, crying, rage, deadpan, hype…), each usable as a meme.
4. Flaws over perfection: anxious, clumsy, tries too hard (Chiikawa, Kuromi).
5. Thin lore with room to project: a name, a home, 2–3 friends, a running gag.
6. A power-up / "awakened" form for boss fights and big moments (competence fantasy, evolution payoff).
7. A rarity ladder (common → variant → rare → secret chase), earned, never real-money randomness for minors.
8. No "bacon hair" default: the starting look must already look good; free, earnable style.
9. Customization pieces + "matching with friends" sets.
10. Absurd humor kids can join in on; never let the brand try to be the meme.
11. Snappy, squashy 2D/chibi timing for reactions.
12. 2D-first (stickers, emotes); a soft vinyl/plush 3D version later; avoid realistic 3D.
