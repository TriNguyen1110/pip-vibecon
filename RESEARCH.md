## Supporting evidence

- **Expecting to teach** improves recall and organization: Nestojko et al. 2014,
  *Memory & Cognition* 42(7) — https://source.washu.edu/2014/07/expecting-to-teach-enhances-learning-recall/ ✅
- **Teaching beats preparing to teach** for long-term retention: Fiorella & Mayer 2013,
  *Contemp. Educ. Psych.* 38(4). Effect size 0.77 is quoted secondhand by EdSurge —
  https://edsurge.com/news/2019-01-24-want-students-to-remember-what-they-learn-have-them-teach-it 🟡
- **Self-explanation effect:** Chi et al. 1994, *Cognitive Science* 18(3) —
  https://pcl.sitehost.iu.edu/rgoldsto/courses/cogscilearning/chiselfexplanation.pdf ✅
- **Teens + ChatGPT for schoolwork doubled** 13% → 26% (2023→2024): Pew —
  https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/01/15/about-a-quarter-of-us-teens-have-used-chatgpt-for-schoolwork-double-the-share-in-2023/ ✅
- **Why we don't adapt to "learning styles":** no rigorous evidence for it (Pashler et al.
  2008, *PSPI* 9(3)) — https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1765/ ✅. We adapt to how
  the student is *responding* instead. (Good answer if a judge asks.)

## Motivation design evidence (gathered Oct 24)

- **Intrinsic integration:** in Zombie Division, children (7–11) learned more from the
  version where the math *was* the game mechanic, and in free time chose it about **7×
  longer** (Study 2, n = 16). Habgood & Ainsworth 2011, *J. Learning Sciences* 20(2) —
  https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/ ✅ (abstract; small sample, say "in one study")
- **Expected rewards undermine intrinsic motivation** (128 studies). Deci, Koestner &
  Ryan 1999, *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6) —
  https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf ✅
- **IKEA effect:** people value things more when they built them. Norton, Mochon &
  Ariely 2012, *J. Consumer Psychology* 🟡
- **Curiosity:** highest when partly known; curious answers remembered better. Kang et
  al. 2009, *Psychological Science*; Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath 2014, *Neuron* 🟡
- **Pretesting effect:** answering before the lesson helps, even when wrong. Richland,
  Kornell & Kao 2009, *J. Exp. Psych.: Applied* 🟡
- **Interest personalization:** algebra problems tied to students' interests improved
  performance. Walkington 2013, *J. Educational Psychology* 🟡

**The gap:** every big AI plays the tutor and is sold to consumers. Nobody flips the role
(the student teaches the AI) **and** turns that into a tutor-facing gap report and a
center-branded parent update. Pip is the learning layer that tutoring centers'
existing software doesn't have.

## Business model (hypothesis — label as such)

- **Price:** $4 per active student per month, $99/month minimum per center. Same range
  as Oases ($99/25 students) and Khanmigo ($4).
- **Example:** 60-student center ≈ $240/mo.
- **ROI story:** a 120-student center pays $480/mo. Center programs run ~$150–$400+/mo per
  student (#6), so keeping **six** students who would otherwise quit pays for it even at
  $80/mo, with a wide margin. Everything else (tutor time, parent trust, upsell
  as a "between-session practice" add-on) is upside.
- **First 100 customers:** independent centers first (no franchise approval needed):
  owner Facebook groups and r/Tutoring, National Tutoring Association members
  (https://www.ntatutor.com/about.html), integration/marketplace partnership with
  center software (Oases, TutorBird, Teachworks). Free 30-day pilot in exchange
  for a case study. Franchises (Mathnasium/Kumon/Sylvan/Huntington, ~4,000 units) later,
  top-down.
